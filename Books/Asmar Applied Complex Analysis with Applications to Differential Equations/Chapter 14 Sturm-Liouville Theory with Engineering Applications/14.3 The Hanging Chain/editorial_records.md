## 1. Restore source wording; retain only the actual correction.

### Before

````text
For a nonzero bounded spatial mode satisfying $X(L)=0$, integration by parts in $(xX')'=\lambda X$ gives

$$
\lambda\int_0^L X^2\,dx=-\int_0^L x(X')^2\,dx<0.
$$

The endpoint term vanishes at $L$ and, for the regular bounded solution, at $0$. Equality would force $X$ to be constant and then zero. Thus the separating constant must be negative.
````
### After

````text
If $\lambda \geq 0$, the solutions of the differential equation in $T$ are either linear or exponential functions. The bounded spatial solutions satisfying $X(L)=0$ are zero in these cases. Thus $\lambda$ must be negative.
````
